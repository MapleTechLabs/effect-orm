Postgres enum types and unique constraints are first-class. `PG.enumType(name, labels, { schema? })` is a column type (rows decode to its labels; inserts and comparisons take only them; `nullable` and `array` wrap it, and enum arrays decode from the text form drivers send) and a schema object: `generate` creates the type before the tables that use it, adds labels with `ADD VALUE ... BEFORE`, renames one behind a `confirm_rename` hint, asks before dropping the type, and reports removing or reordering labels, or using a label in the migration that adds it, as unsupported. `PG.unique(name, columns, { nullsNotDistinct? })` in a table's `uniqueConstraints`, or `unique` in `PG.column` (named `<table>_<column>_unique` by default), declares an `ADD CONSTRAINT ... UNIQUE` constraint, diffed as a constraint rather than an index. `verify` reads enums from `pg_enum` and unique constraints from `pg_constraint`, and resolves types of the current schema during its scratch rebuild, so `PG.custom("public.x", ...)` enums no longer report false drift. `generate --baseline --from-drizzle` converts drizzle-kit enums, enum columns and unique constraints.
